Content Refresh Prioritization

Can clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position tell a content team which pages to look at first? A rule-based baseline and a decision tree were framed and evaluated on the same split — this paper reports what's confirmed so far, and what's still pending.

Abstract

Can search performance metrics — clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position — be used to identify which webpages should be prioritized for content refresh? This project frames the question as a ranking task: page-month records from March 2026 are scored by a rule-based baseline and, in parallel, by a decision tree trained on the same four signals, with both approaches held to an identical 80/20 evaluation split. Label-derived and future-looking fields were excluded during a leakage review before any model was trained, so the comparison reflects signals a team would actually have in hand at review time. The clearest pattern so far is directional rather than final: pages combining high impressions with low CTR repeatedly surface as refresh candidates, while average position adds ranking context on top of that signal. These findings are decision-support evidence for a human reviewer, not a validated accuracy comparison — that comparison is the next step once client-holdout evaluation is complete.

01

Problem statement

Content teams managing large page inventories can't manually re-audit every URL every month. Some pages are quietly losing visibility, some are ranking well but not converting clicks, and most are simply fine — without a structured way to triage, review time goes wherever someone happens to look, not where it's actually needed.

This project asks whether four signals a search team already has — clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position — are enough to rank pages by refresh priority. The decision it supports: which pages get a human content review this cycle, and on what basis.

Methodology

Workflow

Problem framing → data analysis → feature and leakage checks → baseline construction → model training → validation → action recommendations, in that order.

Model choice

A decision tree was chosen for interpretability — it exposes which of the four signals actually drives a flag, rather than acting as a black box.

Baseline

A rule-based prioritization built directly from the search/content performance signals, applied with no fitting, evaluated on the same split as the model.

Leakage check

Label-derived and future-looking fields were identified and excluded before training, so the comparison reflects only information available at review time.

05

Limitations & honest framing

Every claim above is decision-support evidence, not a validated result. Specifically:

  • Decision-support

    The model does not guarantee improved rankings — it flags candidates for review, nothing more.

  • Decision-support

    The model does not predict or model Google's search algorithm; no causal refresh-impact claim is made anywhere in this project.

  • Directional

    Results depend on the available dataset and a single month's window — they may not generalize to other periods without re-validation.

  • Directional

    Additional content-quality signals (beyond clicks, impressions, CTR, and position) could meaningfully improve future versions of this work.

  • Decision-support

    The project was evaluated on internship-provided data, not production data — treat every number as a demo-scale signal, not an operating benchmark.

06

Ranked recommendations

In the order a content team should apply them:

  1. Refresh pages with high impressions but low CTR first — this is the strongest recurring pattern in the data.

  2. Update outdated content that shows declining performance over the trailing window.

  3. Improve titles and meta descriptions on flagged pages to increase click opportunity without a full content rewrite.

  4. Review keyword alignment and user intent for pages that rank well but convert few clicks.

  5. Monitor performance monthly and retrain the approach as new data becomes available — a human reviews every flag before action is taken.
